\repsec{1. \ The talk \hfill \normalfont\small 2--3 sentences}
\hint{Title, speaker, session. Who are the decision makers? What problem are they trying
to solve, and for whom? Why does it matter?}
\blank{3.6em}

\repsec{2. \ The problem as an optimization model \hfill \normalfont\small the core of the report}

\textbf{We decide} \dotfill
\hint{the decision variables --- what gets committed? Which decisions are yes/no (binary),
which are counts (integer), which are amounts (continuous)?}
\blank{2.4em}

\textbf{to minimize / maximize} \dotfill
\hint{the objective --- the one number being improved. If the talk mixes several goals,
pick the one you would optimize and say what happens to the others.}
\blank{2.4em}

\textbf{subject to} \dotfill
\hint{the constraints --- what limits the decisions? budget or capacity,
logic (if this, then that), rules and policy, physics of the system.
Two to four constraints, each one sentence.}
\blank{3.6em}

\textbf{from the data} \dotfill
\hint{what must be known before the model can be written down --- costs, demands, distances,
a network?}
\blank{2.4em}

\repsec{3. \ Analysis \hfill \normalfont\small 4--6 sentences}
\hint{Is their problem genuinely challenging from an optimization perspective --- and what
makes it so (discrete decisions, scale, uncertainty, competing objectives)? Do you see a
mismatch between their actual problem and the optimization model they propose, and how
would you improve their approach? Do they consider deterministic data that should have
been random --- would you consider stochastic data instead? Is there a place where sharper
modeling --- integer decisions, an explicit objective --- would beat their solution?}
\blank{5.2em}
